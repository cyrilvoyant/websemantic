# Qualifiers and assumptions

"A lot", "few" or "low" have a meaning tied to the parameter, never a universal conversion.

The TLS conventions are in [the table](tls-qualifiers.csv), the [descriptor](../descriptors/tls/descriptor.yaml) and the
[machine contract](../ontology/tls-contract.json). For relative traffic: few = 0.3; usual = 1; a lot = 1.5; a huge
amount = 2, dimensionless. These are scenario conventions, not counts or a physical capacity. The table keeps the
French expressions used in the campaigns; the descriptor also declares their English equivalents.

Each translation keeps the text, the variable, the rule, the value, the unit, the source and the agreement. An
explicit numerical value takes priority. A negation or competing levels call for a clarification. "Old lighting" alone
does not define a precise category. Parameters without a scale keep an explicit interpretation policy.

Local tests cover every declared alias, the agreement and the ambiguities. The campaign also uses new wordings,
changes of context and short answers to a targeted question. For LQL-Equiv, a qualitative dose term is always asked,
never converted.

The reserved cases and their answers are not exposed to the agents under evaluation.
